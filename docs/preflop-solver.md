# 9-handed preflop solver

Our own preflop solver for the game the app is built for: 9-handed tournament tables with a 1bb BB ante, every
stack equal, chip EV. It solves a fixed preflop action tree with vectorized DCFR over the 169 hand classes and
writes the `solver` chart set (`src/data/sets/preflopData.ts`). Postflop play is not solved: every flop the tree
reaches is valued by a realization model fitted to our own heads-up postflop packs (`docs/solver-packs.md`,
solved by `src/solver`, `docs/solver.md`).

Every number in the chart comes from this code, our equity table and our packs. No ranges, frequencies or code
from a commercial or third-party solver or chart are used. Published aggregate frequencies appear only as the
PF-BANDS warning bands (§5.4, §5.5): they are printed next to the results and are never targets. No parameter is
ever changed to move a number into a band.

The section numbers below are the ones the code comments cite (`§2.3`, `§3.4`, `§6.5`, …).

## 1. Game and stages

### 1.1 Game

- **Seats** 0..8 are UTG, UTG+1, UTG+2, LJ, HJ, CO, BTN, SB, BB. The engine is generic over 2..9 seats with
  SB = n − 2 and BB = n − 1, never literals. Preflop order is 0..n−1. Postflop order is SB, BB, then 0..n−3
  (`postRank`); heads-up the SB has the button, so the BB acts first postflop.
- **Blinds and ante**: SB 0.5, BB 1, BB ante 1. The ante is dead money paid from the BB's stack, so the BB's live
  stack is S − 1, as `live(k)` in `src/poker/pushfold.ts` and `openjam.ts`.
- **Stacks**: everyone starts with S. Chip EV, no rake, no ICM.
- **Hands**: the 169 classes with the cached 12,000-sample equity table (`scripts/equity-cache.mjs`):
  `eq[a·169 + b]` is the all-in equity of class a against class b, and `cnt[a·169 + b]` the average number of
  class-b combos compatible with one class-a combo (Σ_b cnt = 1225).

### 1.2 Stages

`STAGE_PRESETS` in `tree.ts`; the shipped Korean labels are `STAGE_LABELS` in `export.ts`.

| Stage | Label | Status | S | Open | SB raise | BB iso vs a limp | 3-bet IP / blinds / BB vs SB (× r_open) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `100bb-2.2` | 100bb · 오픈 2.2bb | shipped | 100 | 2.2 | 3 | 3.5 | 3 / 4 / 3 |
| `100bb-3` | 100bb · 펍 사이즈 오픈 3bb | shipped | 100 | 3 | 3.5 | 3.5 | 3 / 3.5 / 3 |
| `55bb-2.2` | 55bb · 오픈 2.2bb | shipped | 55 | 2.2 | 3 | 3.5 | 3 / 4 / 3 |
| `35bb-2` | 35bb · 오픈 2bb | optional | 35 | 2 | 2.5 | 3 | 3 / 4 / 3 |
| `25bb-2` | (none) | validation only | 25 | 2 | 2.5 | 3 | 3 / 4 / 3 |

Shared by every stage: a squeeze adds 1.0 × r_open per caller in position and 1.25 × from the blinds; 4-bets are
2.3 × the 3-bet when the 4-bettor is in position postflop and 2.5 × out of position; the SB re-raises an iso to
3 × the iso; jams below the 4-bet level exist only at 40bb or less; a non-all-in raise must leave an SPR of at
least 1 after a call; at most 3 players see a flop, 4 when the BB closes.

Why these stages:
- **100bb stands for 100bb and deeper.** The surplus shrink of §3.4 saturates at SPR 6, and at 100bb nothing jams
  below the 4-bet level, so deeper stacks would only move the jam thresholds.
- **55bb** is `STAGE_STACK.mid` in `src/play/policy.ts` and the stack of the `pub-srp-btn-bb-55` pack.
- **`100bb-3`** opens 3bb, the size played at the pub, with blind 3-bets of 3.5× (play mode's pub
  `threeBetSize`). It separates what the ranges do from what the size does.
- **35bb** covers the late 30–40bb stacks. It is optional: it ships only if it passes every gate (after the cold-jam
  fallback of §2.8); otherwise the generator drops it with a printed reason and writes the other stages.
- **25bb** is solved only for PF-OVERLAP (§5.4) against our shortNash 25bb set and is never exported. Below 25bb the
  NASH and SHORT_NASH sets stay the reference.

### 1.3 Blind vs blind and limps

| Spot | Actions |
|---|---|
| SB first in | fold / limp / raise to the SB raise / jam (≤ 40bb) |
| BB vs an SB limp | check / iso (if the size rule allows it) / jam (≤ 40bb). No fold. |
| SB vs the iso | fold / call / re-raise to 3 × iso (if the size rule allows it, else jam) / jam (≤ 40bb) |
| BB vs the SB's re-raise | fold / call / jam |
| Facing a jam | fold / call |

Only the SB limps. There are no open limps from other seats.

## 2. Action tree

`buildTree(cfg)` builds a stage's tree once: decision nodes numbered in DFS pre-order with flat typed arrays for the
solver (node i owns the edges `actStart[i] .. actStart[i+1] − 1`), plus `NodeInfo` and `TermInfo` objects for the
export and the tests.

### 2.0 Configuration

`StageConfig` holds every size above and these switches. Every shipped stage has `coldCall`, `overcall`, `squeeze`
and `sbLimp` on, `cold4: "jam"`, `allinOrder: "priority"`, `mode: "full"` and `valuation: "modelB"`. The other
values exist for tests and ablations:
- `cold4: "none"`: cold players behind a 3-bet may only fold (the fallback of §2.8);
- `allinOrder: "table"`: the old all-in rule (§2.8), for ablations only; the export refuses it;
- `mode: "pushfold"` and `mode: "openjam"` (`pushFoldConfig`, `openJamConfig`): exactly the games of
  `solvePushFold` and `solveOpenJam`, for the reductions of §5.1;
- `openOr3BetConfig()`: the small 100bb test tree (188 nodes) with five switches off: cold calls, overcalls,
  squeezes, SB limps and cold jams. Only the BB flats.

### 2.1 State

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `bet[s]` | What seat s has put into the preflop betting, ante excluded. Starts at SB 0.5, BB 1. |
| `live[s]` | S, or S − 1 for the BB. |
| `raises` | Voluntary raises: 0 unopened (an SB limp does not count), 1 open, 2 3-bet or squeeze, 3 4-bet, 4 5-bet jam. |
| `opener`, `threeBettor`, `lastRaiser` | Seats of raise 1, raise 2 and the last raise. After an SB limp and a BB iso, the opener is the BB. |
| `raiseSeq` | Every raise in order, as seats (used by the all-in answer order). |
| `callers` | Seats that called raise 1, in order (cold callers and overcallers). |
| `called3b`, `allinCalled`, `limped` | Someone called raise 2; an all-in was called; the SB limped. |
| `queue` | Seats still to act. After a raise: every live seat that is not all-in, from the seat after the raiser. |

### 2.2 Size helpers

- `jamTo(s) = min(live[s], max over the other live seats of live[o])`: a jam covers every other live seat.
- `okSize(to)`: `to < jamTo − 1e-9` and `jamTo − to ≥ 1.0 × potAfterCall(to)` (within 1e-9), where
  `potAfterCall = Σ bet + ante + (to − bet[s]) + (to − maxBet)`. A non-all-in raise must leave an SPR of at least 1
  after a call; otherwise the jam replaces it.
- `jamOk`: `jamTo ≤ 40` or `raises ≥ 2`.
- Sizes are rounded to 0.01bb. `r_open` is the current bet of raise 1: the open, the SB raise or the iso.

### 2.3 Node rules

The actor is the first seat in the queue that has neither folded nor gone all-in. The first rule that matches
applies.

- **R1, facing an uncalled all-in:** fold, call. The first call closes the action.
  - A **first-in jam** (the jam is raise 1) is answered in table order after the jammer, as in push/fold.
  - A **re-raising jam** (a 3-bet jam at ≤ 40bb, a 4-bet jam, a cold jam, a 5-bet jam, the SB's re-raise jam) is
    answered in priority order: first the seats whose raise is still live, most recent raise first; then the other
    seats with voluntary chips (an SB limp counts, a BB check does not); then cold seats; the last two groups in
    queue order after the jammer. So a cold caller can no longer call a 4-bet jam and fold out the squeezer
    behind it.
- **R2, an all-in was called:** every seat still to act folds (forced, collapsed by R7).
- **R3, unopened:**
  - BB (only after an SB limp): check, iso if `okSize`, jam if `jamOk`;
  - SB: fold, limp, raise to the SB raise, jam if `jamOk`;
  - others: fold, open, jam if `jamOk`.
- **R4, facing raise 1:**
  - The SB facing the BB's iso: fold, call, re-raise to 3 × iso if `okSize`, jam if `jamOk` or the re-raise fails
    `okSize`.
  - Anyone else, with c callers so far: fold; call if c = 0 (any seat), c = 1 (any seat: a 3-way flop) or c = 2 and
    the actor is the BB (a 4-way flop); a raise to `r_open × mult + c × r_open × (1.25 for the blinds, else 1.0)` if
    `okSize`, where mult is `bbVsSb3` for the BB against an SB raise, `blind3` for the blinds and `ip3` otherwise
    (with c ≥ 1 it is a squeeze); jam if `jamOk` or the raise fails `okSize`.
- **R5, facing a non-all-in 3-bet or squeeze:**
  - the opener or a caller: fold; call if nobody has called the 3-bet yet; a small 4-bet only for the opener in a
    pot without callers or limp, to 3-bet × 2.3 if it is in position against the 3-bettor postflop, else × 2.5, if
    `okSize`; jam;
  - any other seat (a **cold** seat, which has not acted): fold, and jam (`cold4: "jam"`). No cold call and no small
    cold 4-bet.
- **R6, facing a non-all-in 4-bet:** the 3-bettor folds, calls or jams; everyone else folds. A 5-bet is always a jam.
- **R7:** a node with one legal action is not a decision; its only child takes its place.

### 2.4 Terminals

| Terminal | Condition | Valuation |
|---|---|---|
| win | One live seat | §3.2 |
| allin | Two live seats, one all-in and called. Always heads-up, because the first call closes the action. | §3.3 |
| flop | 2–4 live seats, no all-in, action closed | §3.4–3.6 |

A flop terminal carries its pot type (`limp`, `srp`, `3bp`, `4bp`), the live seats in postflop order, the aggressor
(the last raiser; none in a limped pot), the pot P and the SPR, the smallest `(live − bet) / P` of its live seats.
The tree never leaves an SPR below 1 at a flop.

### 2.6 Caps and their bias

| Cap | Bias it introduces |
|---|---|
| A cold seat behind a 3-bet may only fold or jam: no cold call, no small cold 4-bet. | 3-bets carry somewhat more fold equity than in a full tree. Much less than with a fold-only cap (§2.8). |
| Only one player calls a 3-bet or squeeze; a later player may only jam or fold. | Squeeze pots carry slightly more fold equity. |
| At most 3 to a flop, 4 when the BB closes; behind two callers only the BB may call. | No 5-way flops; the SB must fold or squeeze behind two callers. |
| One size per raise level. | No mixing of sizes. Charts stay readable; solvers with several sizes look different. |
| Only the SB limps. | No open limps. |
| Folded players' cards are ignored (no bunching, §4.3). | Late seats' ranges are slightly off after many folds. |
| The first call of a jam closes the action (R1). | No 3-way all-ins: a seat with voluntary chips is folded out when a seat of higher priority calls first. |

The last cap is measured by **PF-FROZEN**: the share of dealt hands that end in such a fold-out. In the latest
generator run (§5.5) it is 0.69% of hands at 100bb-2.2 (0.45% with a raiser folded out), 0.64% at 100bb-3, 1.63% at
55bb, 1.08% at 35bb (cold4 "none"; 3.11% with cold jams and the previous fit) and 2.90% at 25bb, above the 0.5%
warning level everywhere. It grows as stacks get shorter,
because more lines end in jams. 3-way all-ins are a v2 item (§9).

### 2.7 Why this tree

These alternatives were rejected at design time on our own prototype engines. The reasons are structural (who may
flat, who must fold behind a flat), so they do not depend on the all-in rule of the prototypes:
- **BB-only flats** make 3-bet ranges independent of the seat (UTG+1 vs UTG equal to BTN vs UTG) and remove every
  in-position flat, while the pub is a flatting game.
- **Heads-up-only cold calls** (no overcall, no squeeze) force the blinds to fold behind a flat; RFI came out 5–9pp
  wider than the full tree.
- **Any seat may cold call, everyone behind folds**: degenerate (UTG opened 52%).

### 2.8 The all-in rule, cold jams and the fallback

The first prototype let the next seat in table order answer any jam, and that seat's call closed the action. A
cold caller could call the opener's 4-bet jam and auto-fold the squeezer holding a raise, which made flats far too
good; with cold jams, any cold jam let the opener call and fold out the 3-bettor. The priority order of R1 fixes
this. Measured on the design-time prototype (100bb-2.2, 500 iterations, 3-feature fit with α = 0.44, not rerun on
this engine):

| Prototype, 100bb-2.2 | Old rule | Priority | Priority + cold jams + α_mw (shipped shape) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFI UTG / UTG+1 / HJ / CO / BTN | 15.7 / 14.7 / 23.9 / 32.6 / 47.9 | 13.0 / 14.4 / 24.1 / 32.7 / 48.1 | 15.5 / 17.2 / 27.5 / 35.1 / 50.7 |
| UTG+1 vs UTG: call / 3-bet | 19.0 / 8.3 | 0.6 / 8.7 | 2.7 / 6.5 |
| HJ vs UTG: call / 3-bet | 8.4 / 9.7 | 3.2 / 8.1 | 5.2 / 7.0 |

The old rule flooded early flats (19%) and inverted RFI (UTG+1 below UTG). With a fold-only cap behind 3-bets
(middle column), 3-bets got free fold equity; cold jams bring 3-bets vs UTG down to 6.5–7% and widen every RFI by
1.5–3pp. The same engine with the old rule never converged at 35bb.

This engine, run once at implementation time with the prototype's parameters and κ for the last column (3-feature
fit, α = 0.44, the old low-SPR rule, 500 iterations), reproduces it: RFI 15.3 / 16.7 / 27.6 / 35.0 / 50.7, BB vs BTN
defense 66.9% against the prototype's 66.9%, max per-seat gain 3.76 against 3.70 mbb/hand; the early flats differ by
1–2pp (UTG+1 vs UTG calls 1.4% against 2.7%). The two are independent implementations of the same game, so this
cross-checks both.

**Fallback.** If a stage with cold jams fails a gate at 4,000 iterations (§4.6), or passes its gates but its reach
leaves too few spots for PF-DATA-1's counts at every ladder step (`chooseLadder`, §6.5), the generator re-solves it
once with `cold4: "none"`. If that passes, it ships with "3벳 뒤 콜드 올인 없음." in its description and
`tree.cold4: "none"` in the data. The second trigger was added with the 9-feature fit: its 35bb cold-jam solve passes
every gate at 1,500 iterations (max gain 0.328, NashConv 1.95, snapped 1.08 mbb) in the same degenerate equilibrium
the snapped gate used to flag (UTG opens only by jamming, §8), which leaves 28 vs-open spots against PF-DATA-1's
minimum of 30; without it the optional 35bb would be dropped. It changes no gate or count threshold and is inert on a
stage that passes normally (55bb-2.2 solves identically with and without it). In the latest run 35bb-2 shipped on
the fallback (§5.5), so the fallback is the only usable 35bb for now, and its charts carry the fold-only cap's
signature of the middle column above: cold flats all but vanish, 3-bets inflate and the late RFI shrinks (§8).

## 3. Terminal valuation

### 3.1 Chip accounting

T_s = bet[s] + (1 for the BB), the total seat s has put in; P = Σ_s T_s; net_s = chips received − T_s. Folded seats
get −T_s whatever they hold. Σ_s net_s = 0 at every terminal for every hand tuple, and at multiway flops on average
over the normalization (§3.6).

### 3.2 Uncontested

The winner w gets P: net_w = P − T_w.

### 3.3 All-in (heads-up between a and b)

The bigger bettor gets refund = |bet[a] − bet[b]| back, P' = P − refund, and
net_a(h, x) = eq[h, x] · P' + refund_a − T_a, symmetrically for b.

### 3.4 Heads-up flop: model B through the affine pairwise kernel

O is the seat that acts first postflop holding class h, I the other holding x, e = eq[h, x]. O's share of the pot:

    Φ_O(h, x) = (p_O(h) + p_I(x)) · e + q_O(h) − q_I(x)        Φ_I = 1 − Φ_O
    net_O = Φ_O · P − T_O                                      net_I = (1 − Φ_O) · P − T_I

Every pair is zero-sum. One kernel covers every valuation the engine needs:

| Valuation | p_O | p_I | q_O | q_I |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Showdown equity (also every all-in) | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| openjam's flat call, BB acts first: the BB realizes R(h)·e | R(h) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| openjam's flat call, BB acts last: Φ_I = R(x)·(1 − e) | 0 | R(x) | 0 | R(x) − 1 |
| **Model B (shipped)** | 1 + s_α·α | 0 | −s_α·α/2 + s·(c + u_O(h)) | s·u_I(x) |
| Equity-scaled surplus (sensitivity solve (d), §6.6) | R_O(h) | R_I(x) − 1 | 0 | R_I(x) − 1 |

Model B written out:

    Φ_O(h, x) = e + s_α·α·(e − ½) + s·[ c + u_O(h) − u_I(x) ]
    u(h) = θ · F(h),   F = (pair, suited, hi2f5, lo2, gap, pairRank2, aceTop, wheel, suitConn), each centered on its
                       combo-weighted mean
    s   = min(1, SPR / 6)      SPR_SURPLUS = 6: shrink of the position and playability terms c and u
    s_α = min(1, SPR / 1.5)    SPR_ALPHA = 1.5: shrink of the equity-edge term α

The features (`features.ts`, model id `modelB-rank9`; r = 0 for a deuce up to 12 for an ace, hi the top card, lo the
kicker): three rank terms, each a monotone function of one rank (hi2f5, lo2, pairRank2); gap, a function of the
distance hi − lo; and five bounded terms: the indicators pair, suited, aceTop and wheel, and suitConn = suited ×
(1 − gap).

| Feature | Non-pair | Pair | Combo-weighted mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| pair | 0 | 1 | 0.058824 |
| suited | 1 if suited | 0 | 0.235294 |
| hi2f5 | (max(hi, 3) / 12)², rank 3 being the 5 | 0 | 0.511312 |
| lo2 | (lo / 12)² | 0 | 0.143791 |
| gap | min(hi − lo − 1, 4) / 4 | 0 | 0.609351 |
| pairRank2 | 0 | (r / 12)² | 0.020425 |
| aceTop | 1 if the top card is an ace | 0 | 0.144796 (192 / 1326) |
| wheel | 1 for A5–A2, suited or offsuit | 0 | 0.048265 (64 / 1326) |
| suitConn | suited × (1 − gap) | 0 | 0.082956 |

Each column is the raw value minus its mean over the 1,326 combos (`FEATURE_MEANS`: Σ comboCount·raw in `ALL_HANDS`
order, divided once by 1326). Why this form (numbers measured on the 1,476 + 1,457-flop rows, α held, OOP / IP):
- **Top card and kicker are additive.** Ax and Kx with the same kicker 6–8 differ by the same top-card step,
  θ_hi2f5·(1 − (11/12)²) + θ_aceTop (−0.037 OOP / −0.056 IP); with a kicker 2–5 θ_wheel is added (K5s → A5s
  −0.016 / −0.046), and at kicker 9 one gap step and the suitConn step. The packs measure a realization deficit that
  grows steadily with the top card: free top-card dummies in place of hi2f5 and aceTop (the other seven features
  kept) give IP T −0.016, J −0.032, Q −0.059, K −0.096, A −0.148 relative to 9-high (jackknife SE 0.002–0.004); the
  model gives −0.021, −0.045, −0.072, −0.102, −0.158. Relative to the ace, the model is within 0.010 of the dummies
  for every top card from 5 to K (K→A −0.056 against −0.052); the exception is 4-high (+0.018), which is 43s alone.
  The model is still steeper than the dummies from 9 to J and shallower from Q to K (−0.030 against −0.037), so it
  over-states the IP K − Q share step (§3.7, bias 5).
- **Flat at and below 5-high.** No IP pack row is 3-high and 43s (cash pack, 0.3% of the IP weight) is the only
  4-high one, so hi2f5 holds the 5-high value below it instead of continuing the curve (the data rule: flat
  extrapolation where the data stop). The previous hi2 kept rising, which made the BTN open 32s / 42s and 3-bet low
  suited connectors (§3.7, bias 8). Without the floor the CV is the same and IP 32s realizes 1.44 instead of 1.41
  against the cash BB range.
- **aceTop: the ace's own step.** A pure power curve cannot put the largest step at A − K and the smaller ones below
  it: the previous hi2 put −0.039 at A − K where the dummies put −0.054 and was 1.5–2 times too steep from T to Q.
  aceTop is constant across kickers, so u stays monotone in the kicker. θ_aceTop −0.019 ± 0.005 / −0.023 ± 0.003
  (jackknife over 20 flop groups).
- **wheel: A5–A2 realize better than A6.** The packs' own A6 − A5 share gap is negative in all 8 pack / side /
  suitedness cells (−0.015 to −0.019 OOP, −0.002 to −0.009 IP; the model −0.013 to −0.017 OOP, −0.002 to +0.001 IP,
  equity gaps +0.001 to +0.006). θ_wheel +0.022 ± 0.004 / +0.010 ± 0.003. The previous fit under-predicted the OOP
  suited wheel aces by +0.010 to +0.022 of the pot (residual data − model, A5s–A2s, both packs); this one is within
  −0.004 to +0.008. This is the one place where u falls with the kicker (A6 below A5), as PF-SANITY-7 already allows.
- **suitConn: the suited bonus shrinks with connectivity.** suited × (1 − gap): θ −0.025 ± 0.002 / −0.032 ± 0.002, so
  a suited connector keeps 0.049 / 0.038 of the 0.074 / 0.070 suited bonus a suited four-gapper gets. The previous fit
  over-predicted OOP 65s and 76s by 0.021–0.029 of the pot; this one by 0.007–0.016.
- **Monotone by sign.** u rises with the kicker in every row when θ_lo2 ≥ 0, θ_gap ≤ 0 and θ_gap − θ_suitConn ≤ 0 (in
  a suited row gap and suitConn move together), except A6 against A5 (wheel), and with the pair rank when
  θ_pairRank2 ≥ 0. The shipped fit has all four signs on both sides, as do both 2-fold CV fits and the cash-only fit
  (a pub-only fit flips θ_O.pairRank2 to −0.183, §3.7 bias 7). `features.test.ts` fails if a refit breaks one, and
  walks PF-SANITY-7's kicker ladder on u itself.
- **Centering.** A constant added to u_O (or u_I) is exactly collinear with c, so the fit identifies only
  c + mean(u_O) − mean(u_I). Centering puts the whole level in c (−0.078; the 5-feature fit's sum was −0.069). In the
  fitted context it is an exact reparametrization (same θ, same metrics), but the derived contexts read c and u
  separately (the ⅔ / ⅓ split of c, u by role), so it decides what they get: uncentered, the features carry
  (θ_O − θ_I)·`FEATURE_MEANS` = +0.058 of the pot (+0.062 with the previous 6 features) as a level in
  u_call − u_aggr, which the role rule would hand to every IP caller (OOP-aggressor pots) and to the multiway
  u_role − ū term for no reason in the data. Limped pots are not affected: both seats use u_call there, so the level
  cancels. Centering on the mean over all combos is one convention among several: centering u_call on the pack's BB
  range and u_aggr on its BTN range instead (also exact in the fitted context) would move the IP-caller level by
  −0.017 (cash pack ranges) or −0.012 (pub), which is the size of the choice.

Only one context is fitted: a single raised pot with an in-position aggressor (§3.7). Every other context follows
from it by one rule:
- **u by role, not by position.** The fitted OOP vector is the caller's (u_call = θ_O·F), the fitted IP vector the
  aggressor's (u_aggr = θ_I·F). In any heads-up flop the last raiser uses u_aggr and the other player u_call; in a
  limped pot both use u_call.
- **c splits into ⅔ position and ⅓ initiative** (`contextC`). With c_fit = −0.0781: IP aggressor c = c_fit (−0.0781);
  OOP aggressor ⅓·c_fit (−0.0260); limped pot ⅔·c_fit (−0.0520). A negative c moves pot share from O to I.
- **3-bet and 4-bet pots** use the same rule; their lower SPR shrinks c and u through s. At 100bb-2.2 an IP 3-bet
  pot has s ≈ 1, a blind 3-bet about 0.8 and a 4-bet pot 0.3–0.4.
- **α is shared** by every context and shrinks only below SPR 1.5. Since no flop has an SPR below 1, s_α is between
  ⅔ and 1 everywhere. α itself is held at the prior 0.553, not fitted (§3.7).

only through Φ moving away from e. If α shrank with s, a strong hand in a called small 4-bet (SPR 2–3) would earn

for the weaker hand, but nothing suggests it falls below the α measured at SPR 7.9–17.7 while 2·SPR > α. As SPR → 0

### 3.5 Kernel CFV formulas

With K[h, x] = cnt[h, x] / 1225 (the chance a given class-x combo fits with a class-h combo; K is not symmetric) and
KE[h, x] = K[h, x] · eq[h, x], against the opponent's reach vector r:

    O holding h:  M = Σ_x K·r_I,  A1 = Σ_x KE·r_I,  A2 = Σ_x KE·r_I·p_I,  B = Σ_x K·r_I·q_I
                  CFV_O(h) = P·[ p_O(h)·A1 + A2 + q_O(h)·M − B ] − T_O·M
    I holding x:  M' = Σ_h K·r_O,  C1 = Σ_h K·r_O·p_O,  C2 = Σ_h KE·r_O·p_O,  D1 = Σ_h KE·r_O,  Q = Σ_h K·r_O·q_O
                  CFV_I(x) = P·[ M' − (C1 − C2) − p_I(x)·(M' − D1) − Q + q_I(x)·M' ] − T_I·M'

Each side is one fused loop over the opponent's non-zero classes: 4 and 5 accumulators in general, 3 each when p_O
is constant and p_I is 0 (model B, equity). An all-in needs only M and A1 (2 accumulators). The normalized value is
V(h) = CFV(h) / M(h), and 0 where M ≤ 1e-300. Measured on one 169×169 side (`value.test.ts`, printed): dense reach
40.6 µs for K·r, 53 µs for an all-in side, 89 / 110 µs for model B's O / I side; with 85% zeros in the reach,
5–20 µs.

### 3.6 Multiway flop (3–4 live seats)

For live seat s holding h, with ē_o(h) = A1_o(h) / M_o(h) its equity against seat o's reach:

    raw_s(h)   = κ_k(h) · Π_{o≠s} ē_o(h) / Z
    share_s(h) = raw_s(h) + s_α·α_mw·(raw_s(h) − 1/k) + s·( c_pos(s) + u_role(s)(h) − ū )
    net_s      = share_s · P − T_s

- **κ_k(h)**, k = 3, 4: the Monte Carlo k-way pot share of class h divided by the product of its pairwise equities
  (§3.7). At design time the κ-corrected product measured 0.011 mean absolute error in pot share (0.018 out of
  sample) against 0.052 for the plain product.
- **α_mw** carries model B's equity-edge term to k-way pots, with raw_s − 1/k in place of e − ½. Prior: α_mw = α.
  Without it a medium hand got raw equity as soon as a pot went multiway, while heads-up it lost α(e − ½); that
  inflated flats and overcalls.
- **c_pos**: −0.02 for the first to act, +0.02 for the last, 0 in between (`MW_C_POS`). **u_role**: u_aggr for the
  raiser, u_call for everyone else.
- **Z and ū**: Z = Σ_s of the reach-weighted mean over h of κ_k(h)·Π_{o≠s} ē_o(h), seat s's weights
  r_s(h)·CW(h)·Π_{o≠s} M_o(h); ū = the mean over the live seats of their reach-weighted mean u_role. Both are
  computed from the **average** strategy σ̄ at iterations 1, 26, 51, … and frozen in between, so the payoff is a fixed
  function of the hand tuple and the game stays multilinear (a payoff that depends on a player's current range
  diverges under CFR). Z ≤ 0.2 (a terminal with almost no reach) is replaced by 1.
- Cost: one M, A1 pass per live seat. 140 of the 6,024 terminals at 100bb are multiway.

Multiway realization cannot be calibrated from our packs, which are heads-up. It stays the κ-corrected product of
pairwise equities (raw equity, no realization edge) plus the priors above.

### 3.7 Realization: the fit

`scripts/fit-realization.mjs` fits the heads-up model to our own flop packs and writes `eqrFit.ts`.

**Extraction.** For every flop file of every pack in `public/packs/manifest.json` (or of the packs named by
`--packs <id,id,...>`; default all), its sha256 checked against the manifest, decoded with the app's decoder:
1. OOP's root node `[]` and IP's node after OOP checks give each combo's EV, Σ_a strategy · ev. The packs give OOP
   no flop bet; the script asserts OOP checks at least 99.9% of its reach-weighted range (measured: 100%), since
   IP's root EV is not stored otherwise.
2. Each combo's flop equity against the opponent's stored range, by full turn and river enumeration.
3. Per pack, side and class, with w = node weight × file weight: share = Σ w·EV / Σ w / pot and e = Σ w·eq / Σ w.
   Every class with Σ w > 0 is a row.

**Fit.** Model B is linear in its parameters at class level:

    OOP row h:  share − e = α(e − ½) + c + θ_O·F(h) − θ_I·E_x[F(x)]
    IP row x:   share − e = α(e − ½) − c + θ_I·F(x) − θ_O·E_h[F(h)]

E is over the opponent's pack range (its manifest range string) with card removal through `cnt`, the engine's own
pairwise model. α is held at `ALPHA_PRIOR` = 0.553: α(e − ½) moves into the offset and only c, θ_O and θ_I are
fitted (19 parameters: c and 9 + 9 θ). Weighted least squares, ridge 1e-4 on every fitted parameter, all packs
jointly; a row's weight is its Σ w, normalized so each side of each pack sums to 1, times the pack's flop count. The
joint, cash-only, pub-refit and both CV-fold fits all hold α the same way. The same rows fit three alternatives:
model B on the first 3 features (pair, suited, hi2f5), α held (7 parameters, sensitivity solve (c)); the
equity-scaled surplus form share_O = R_O(h)·e − E_x[(R_I(x) − 1)(1 − eq[h, x])] with R(h) = r0 + θ·F(h) on all 9
features (20 parameters, sensitivity solve (d)); and model B with α fitted too (20 parameters, `alphaFree`, a
diagnostic no solve uses).

**Why α is held.** Once the features can follow the ranks, the class rows pin α down only weakly. Freed, every
subset now puts it below 0: −0.177 jointly (`alphaFree`), −0.169 on the cash pack, −0.313 on the pub pack, −0.080 and
−0.185 on the two CV training folds (the previous 6-feature fit on 468 + 448 flops gave +0.078; 0.955 with a graded
top-card feature set). A negative α does not make a hand's share fall as its equity edge grows: the share still
rises, with slope 1 + α (0.82 at −0.177, 1.55 at 0.553). It makes the realization share / e fall with equity before c
and u (at α −0.177 from 1.08 at e 0.35 to 0.96 at e 0.65, against 0.76 to 1.13 at 0.553), so the features carry the
whole rise of realization with hand strength: freed, θ_pairRank2 grows from 0.209 / 0.305 to 0.548 / 0.543. That
also flattens the small-pair curve the packs measure (bias 9). The CV RMS (OOP / IP) is 0.0168 / 0.0137 free, 0.0164 / 0.0152 held at 0.2, 0.0167 / 0.0174 at 0.45,
0.0170 / 0.0185 at 0.553 and 0.0185 / 0.0231 at 0.95: holding 0.553 costs the IP side about 0.005 of CV RMS and the
OOP side 0.0002. α matters to the engine: it decides the low-SPR 4-bet /
jam split (§3.4), and a 7-feature variant with a free α of 0.23 tripled PF-SANITY-6 at 100bb (7 → 27 spots). So α
stays at 0.553, the 5-feature fit's value, held deliberately for the low-SPR split as a named prior (§3.8) until a
3-bet-pot or ghost-combo calibration pack (§9) can measure it. It also moves the deep charts, and no sensitivity
solve varies it there (§3.8): two model-selection solves of that 7-feature variant at 100bb-2.2, α 0.231 against
0.553, gave BB defense vs the BTN 87.2 against 84.5%, CO RFI 35.3 against 36.2% and PF-SANITY-6 27 against 13 spots.

**Known biases of the calibration:**
1. **The pack trees favour IP.** OOP has no flop bet and only a 60% check-raise; IP bets 33% or 75% and cannot

## 4. Algorithm

### 4.1 Representation

`createSolver` keeps regrets and strategy sums as two `Float64Array(edgeCount · 169)`, node i's action a at
`(actStart[i] + a) · 169`. Terminals hold their kernel vectors, P, T_s and the live seats. Scratch vectors are
allocated once per depth; nothing is allocated per node per iteration. A node computes values only for the seats
that acted above it (the only ones whose regrets read them), and every kernel and K·r product skips zero entries of
the reach vector, which regret matching makes plentiful.

### 4.2 One iteration (simultaneous DCFR)

Every seat t has its own reach vector r_t (the product of its own action probabilities along the line) and a mass
vector m_t(h) = Σ_x K[h, x] · r_t(x): the chance that t holds a hand consistent with its actions, seen by an observer
holding h. At the root r_t = m_t = 1. At a decision node of seat s:

1. Regret matching: σ_a(h) = R⁺_a(h) / Σ_b R⁺_b(h), uniform when no regret is positive.
2. For each non-fold action: r_s^a = r_s · σ_a and m_s^a = K · r_s^a. The fold's mass is
   max(0, m_s − Σ_{a≠F} m_s^a), with no product.
3. Recurse into each child with r_s, m_s replaced.
4. Values: V_s = Σ_a σ_a · V_s^a for the actor; V_t = Σ_a (m_s^a / m_s) · V_t^a for every other seat t (0 where
   m_s ≤ 1e-300).
5. Update with w(h) = Π_{t≠s} m_t(h), the counterfactual reach of s's class h, card removal included:
   R_a ← d(R_a) + w · (V_s^a − V_s) and S_a ← S_a · g + r_s · σ_a.

DCFR discounts at iteration t, applied before adding, as in `src/solver`: α = 1.5, β = 0, γ = 2, so
d(R) = R · t^1.5 / (t^1.5 + 1) for R > 0, R · 0.5 otherwise, and g = (t / (t + 1))². t^1.5 is computed as t · √t and
g as q · q, never `Math.pow`, so runs are bit-reproducible. All seats update in the same pass (simultaneous, not
alternating, which would cost 9 passes per iteration). At a terminal, live seats get the kernel values of §3,
folded seats −T_s.

### 4.3 Card removal: the mass method

Card removal is pairwise between the observer and each other seat; folded seats enter through their
card-removal-adjusted fold mass; live opponents' hands are independent of each other given the observer's hand;
there is no bunching. This is push/fold's own approximation (`reach *= 1 − pc` in `pushGain`), generalized to the
tree, which is why the reductions of §5.1 are exact.

**With 3 or more seats it is not one well-defined game.** Each seat weights a line by the other seats' masses
conditioned on its own hand, so each seat sees a slightly different joint distribution of hands. Every terminal
conserves chips for every hand tuple, but the seats' root EVs do not sum to 0: Σ EV is +0.12 to +0.13 bb/hand in
every stage of the latest run (§5.5). At design time the same approximation gave +0.13 bb/hand on the 10bb push/fold
and 20bb open/jam games, the games of `pushfold.ts` and `openjam.ts`, which share it; without card removal the sum
was 0 within 1e-14. What stays valid: each seat's best response and gain, computed in that seat's own
world, and NashConv as their sum. What is not exact: the per-seat EV (`PreflopStage.ev` is each seat's own-world
EV) and any Σ EV = 0 test with card removal and 3+ seats. Conservation is therefore tested only where it is exact
(PF-CONS-1), and Σ EV is printed as the "model inconsistency" diagnostic.

### 4.4 Average strategy and the hero's reach

σ̄_a(h) = S_a(h) / Σ_b S_b(h), uniform where the sum is 0. At export the hero's reach at a node is
ρ(h) = Π of the **snapped** frequencies (§6.5) of the hero's own earlier actions, read from the exported scenarios
where it took them (`reachFrom`). A hand is in reach iff ρ(h) > 0, the same test the app applies, so a hand never
shows frequencies while dimmed or the reverse; frequencies of hands out of reach are written as 0. A node is
exported only if every earlier decision of its hero is exported, so `reachFrom` is always complete.

### 4.5 Metrics

`solver.evaluate(strategy)` refreshes Z and ū from the strategy it evaluates (and restores the training run's
afterwards, so checking never changes a solve), then computes:
- **Per-seat best-response gain**: the walk with every seat on the strategy except seat s, which takes max_a per
  class at its own nodes; gain_s = BR value − value at the root, combo-weighted, mbb/hand, in seat s's own world.
  **NashConv** = Σ_s gain_s.
- **Reach-weighted local regret** at node n: Σ_h CW(h)·ρ(h)·(max_a V^a(h) − Σ_a σ_a(h)·V^a(h)) / Σ_h CW(h)·ρ(h), in bb
  per reaching combo, with CW = 6 / 4 / 12 combos per class and ρ the raw (unsnapped) own reach.
- **occ(n)**: the chance the node's public line occurs in a dealt hand, Π_t Σ_h CW(h)·r_t(h) / 1326.
- **Snapped gain**: the per-seat gain against the profile in which exported nodes use the snapped strategy and every
  other node σ̄.
- Diagnostics, never gates: **PF-FROZEN** (Σ occ over terminals where R1 folded out a seat with voluntary chips,
  split into raisers and callers; warning above 0.5%); **negative shares** (reaching (terminal, seat, class) triples
  whose range-averaged flop share is below 0: model B is linear, so a weak hand against a strong range can get one,
  where a real player would check-fold and get at least 0); **Σ EV** (§4.3).

### 4.6 Schedule and gates

| Gate (`DEFAULT_GATES`) | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Max per-seat BR gain | ≤ 1.0 mbb/hand |
| NashConv | ≤ 5 mbb/hand |
| Every exported node | reach-weighted local regret ≤ 0.05 bb |
| Snapped profile | max per-seat gain ≤ 3 mbb/hand (warning above 2) |

There is no conservation gate (§4.3). `runSchedule` runs 1,500 iterations and checks; while a gate fails it runs 250
more, up to 4,000. The verdict is the check at the end, never an earlier good reading (prototypes oscillated). If at
4,000 only the local-regret gate fails, a **polish pass** freezes σ̄ outside the failing nodes' subtrees, resets the
strategy sums inside them, restarts the discount counter and runs 300 DCFR iterations on those subtrees alone for
the frozen incoming reach, then checks again. In the generator the exported set at each check is the size ladder of
§6.5 applied to that check's occ.

A stage that still fails gets the `cold4: "none"` re-solve (§2.8), and so does a cold-jam stage whose main solve
passes but whose reach cannot meet PF-DATA-1's counts at any ladder step. If the re-solve fails too, an optional
stage is dropped with a printed reason and a required one (100bb-2.2, 100bb-3, 55bb-2.2) fails the run: exit 1,
nothing written.

### 4.8 FP mode (tests only)

`createSolver(…, { mode: "fp" })` runs the fictitious play of `solvePushFold` and `solveOpenJam`: each iteration every
node takes the best response to the current average with the reference's tie rules, then σ̄ += (BR − σ̄)/(t + 2).
It exists to compare dynamics with the reference solvers (PF-RED-3); the charts use DCFR.

### 4.9 Generic sizes

The engine is generic over the class count N (169, or 3 in a toy test) and the seat count n (2..9); nothing hard-codes
seat 7 or 8. The equity and compatibility matrices, the features and the fitted parameters are inputs, not imports.

### 4.10 Determinism

- No randomness in CFR; the traversal order is fixed; discounts avoid `Math.pow`.
- κ comes from seeded mulberry32 streams in the fit script, and every sum there runs in a fixed order.
- The equity table is built in `SHARDS = 4` seeded shards whatever the machine's core count (it used to follow the
  CPU count, which made every chart depend on the machine). Its sha256 is recorded in `eqrFit.ts` and in every stage.
- **The table is noisy.** 12,000 samples per class pair give a standard error of about 0.45pp; against a 1M-sample
  check (review) AA vs KK reads 82.59 against 82.00, 77 vs AKo 54.43 against 54.99, and 66 vs AKo (55.04) sits above
  77 vs AKo. In a 200bb all-in pot 0.5pp is about 1bb, so which of two neighbouring pairs jams or calls in a 100bb
  all-in spot (55 and 66 jam, 77 and 88 fold in `bb-at-btn-open-sb-3bet`) is decided by noise. A more precise table
  (≥ 200k samples per pair, or exact heads-up enumeration) changes the table hash, so it needs a refit and a full
  regeneration.
- The data file is a pure function of its inputs: no timestamps, fixed ordering, independent of the worker count.
  `make-preflop.mjs --check` solves `35bb-2` twice at 300 iterations and compares σ̄ and the stage JSON (PF-DET), and
  PF-DET-1 checks the engine bit for bit in vitest.

### 5.3 Generator gates

The §4.6 gates per stage, with the cold-jam fallback and the optional/required rule. The snapped gain is checked
again on the final export set (a gzip step changes it) with the same per-stage rule: an optional stage over it is
dropped and the other stages restart from their count ladders; a required one fails the run (there is no cold4
re-solve at that point). Any failed data check, reproduction check or PF-SANITY-1/2/3 failure also fails the run
(nothing written), unless `--ignore-gates`. So does an equity table whose sha256 is not the one `eqrFit.ts` was
fitted on, and a `--stages` subset that leaves out a shipped stage without an explicit `--out`.

They are third-party aggregates and the only third-party numbers in this document. They are generic, not made for

error. Nothing is ever tuned to them.

**PF-OVERLAP.** The 25bb solve against our shortNash 25bb set (same 2bb open): open + jam per seat within 5pp, BB
defense difference printed. The trees are not the same game (ours has flats from any seat, small 3- and 4-bets and
the R1 order), so this is a loose check.

**PF-TWOSTART.** Each shipped stage is solved a second time from a different start: fold regret +1 and every other
regret 0 at every node with a fold, 50 iterations, then strategy sums reset and the discount counter restarted, then
the normal schedule. At every candidate node with reach ≥ 1%, the action shares of the two solves (each of its own
reaching range) must agree within 2pp; a node that does not is badged 낮음. This is the only practical test for
multiple equilibria in a 9-player game.

**PF-FROZEN, negative shares, Σ EV**: the diagnostics of §4.5, per stage.

**PF-ABLATE** (manual): rerun 100bb-2.2 with one switch changed at a time (`allinOrder: "table"`, `cold4: "none"`,
α_mw = 0, SPR_ALPHA = 6, `maxFlop: 2`, no SB limp) and record the deltas. Not yet run on this engine; the prototype
numbers of §2.8 stand until it is.

## 6. Output and app integration

### 6.1 Generator flow

`scripts/make-preflop.mjs`, each solve in its own worker thread, at most `--workers` at once (default min(4, CPUs)):
1. Per stage, the main solve on the §4.6 schedule; on a gate failure the `cold4: "none"` re-solve; then the
   optional/required rule. 25bb-2 stops here (PF-OVERLAP only).
2. Per shipped stage, PF-TWOSTART and the five sensitivity solves (1,000 iterations each, cold start).
3. The size ladder (§6.5), the export (§6.2–6.6), the data checks (§5.2), the reproduction check, the snapped gain on
   the final export set. The ladder and the gate run on the data without annotations.
4. The close calls and flags (§6.9, §6.10) on the final export sets: per stage one evaluation of σ̄ with the
   best-response action values (about 0.8 s), then `annotateStage`; PF-SIZE-1 on the annotated file, with the close
   fallback of §6.9. They never change which spots ship.
5. Summaries per stage (`summary-<stage>.txt`, with the marked cells and the flags per code), then the data file:
   written, or with `--check` compared (exit 1 if it would change). `--check`'s PF-DET compares the annotated export
   of its two solves, so the determinism check covers the annotations.

### 6.2 Generated data format

`src/data/sets/preflopData.ts` starts with a header comment saying it is generated by `scripts/make-preflop.mjs` and
must not be edited, imports its types (`import type { PreflopStage } from "./preflopTypes";`) and exports
`PREFLOP_DRAFT_STAGES` (stages written by a development run; empty in a release run) and
`PREFLOP_STAGES: PreflopStage[]`, written with `JSON.stringify(…, null, 1)`. The shape is
`src/data/sets/preflopTypes.ts`, hand-written and shared by the generator, the chart set and the tests. Per stage:
id, label, Korean description, stack, open, every size, `tree.cold4`, iterations, `occThreshold` (the lowest
threshold, 0.1) and `occThresholdByKind`, `occThresholdCold` (the cold vs3bet spots' own threshold), per-seat gains, NashConv, snapped
gain, per-seat own-world EV (whose sum is not 0, §4.3), the diagnostics, the model (`modelB-rank9`, fit hash,
SPR_ALPHA, SPR_SURPLUS, pack versions and flop counts), the equity table's sha256, `closeEps` (the close-call threshold,
0.05 bb per arrival), `closeMinOcc` (only on a stage that PF-SIZE-1's fallback took close strings from: spots below it carry none)
and the scenarios. Per scenario: id, kind, hero, villain(s), chip, title,
description, public line, `reachFrom`, `reachPct`, `noFold`, occ (% of dealt hands), local regret, confidence,
move, `close` (§6.9: 169 characters in grid order, "." or a hex bitmask over the legend; omitted when no hand is
marked), `flags` (§6.10: `{ code, hands, vs? }`, range strings; omitted when none) and the non-fold actions with their
range strings. Fold is always the remainder. The new keys sit before `actions` and `scenarios`, so the rest of the file
reads as before.

### 6.3 The chart set

`src/data/sets/preflop.ts` builds the `solver` set ("솔버 프리플랍 (9인·BB 앤티)") from the data without changing a
range or a text. It adds the hero's `reachRange` (ρ from `reachFrom`), `weight` = min(occ, 5) for quiz picks (0 for the
fold-or-jam cold spots of stages of 55bb and more: real players cold 4-bet small there, so the tree's answers such as
"fold QQ, jam A4s" must not be drilled; the charts page still shows them),
`noFold` for the BB facing an SB limp, the hero-seat filter for big kind groups, `close` decoded to legend keys per
marked hand (`closeActions`) with the stage's `closeEps`, the `flags` as written, their Korean cautions
(`cautionLines`, `flagLines`, §6.10) and `quizSkip`: the quiz never deals a flag's `hands`, nor the `vs` of
PF-SANITY-9 and -14, where both sides of the comparison are distorted. The `vs` of -11 and -13 stay in the quiz: TT–77
there are mostly ties, and AA's small 4-bet is the line §8 recommends. A spot whose hands are all skipped falls back
to its reaching hands.

The set is built on first use. `CHART_SETS` holds the four hand-made sets (built at load; `CHART_SETS[0]` is `pub`,
the default chart set and `getSet`'s fallback). The solver set sits in `LAZY_SETS` and is built once, synchronously,
by the first `getSet("solver")`, `allSets()`, `allScenarios()` without a set, or `getNamedRanges()` (the calculator's
villain ranges, memoized; a set pushed into `CHART_SETS` later is not added). The set picker reads `chartSetMenu()`
(the solver set last) and `chartSetId(id)`, so listing it builds nothing. Before, every app start built it and its
calculator ranges: in Chromium (iPhone 13 viewport, the built app, median of 5) the pub home screen's first render
took 478 ms at 1× CPU and 1,601 ms at 4×, with 38.0 MB of JS heap; now 219 ms, 843 ms and 17.6 MB. The first solver
screen costs 295 ms (1×) / 1,187 ms (4×) and 25.7 MB; switching to the solver set the first time 161 / 605 ms, the
second time 15 / 64 ms. In Node the first `getSet("solver")` takes about 120 ms and holds 15 MB, the first
`getNamedRanges()` about 110 ms and 7 MB more. There is no separate chunk: the data stays in the main bundle (about 1.30 MB minified, under workbox's 2 MiB precache
limit). `src/data/lazySolver.test.ts` checks that the menu, the other sets, the pub quiz pool and the pub charts and
quiz pages do not build it. Hand-play bots and grading do not use it in v1 (§9).

### 6.4 Scenario mapping

`classifyNode` gives every decision node a kind, an id, a Korean title, a chip and its exported actions. It depends
on the tree only, and throws on a node that matches no row, so a tree change cannot slip in an unnamed spot. Seat
ids are lowercase with "+" → "p" (`utgp1`). The first matching row wins:

| Kind | Spot | Id |
|---|---|---|
| rfi | First in, UTG–SB | `rfi-<seat>` |
| vsLimp | Any decision in a limped pot | `bb-vs-sb-limp` (no fold), `sb-limp-vs-bb-iso`, `sb-limp-vs-bb-jam`, `bb-iso-vs-sb-reraise`, `bb-iso-vs-sb-jam`, `sb-reraise-vs-bb-jam` |
| vsOpen | Facing a non-all-in open, no caller, hero has not acted (includes the BB vs an SB raise) | `<hero>-vs-<opener>-open` |
| vsShove | Facing a first-in jam, hero has not acted | `<hero>-vs-<jammer>-jam` |
| vs3bet | Opener facing a non-all-in 3-bet, no callers | `<opener>-open-vs-<3bettor>-3bet` |
| vs3bet | Opener facing a 3-bet jam, no callers | `<opener>-open-vs-<jammer>-jam` |
| vs4bet | 3-bettor facing the opener's small 4-bet | `<3bettor>-3bet-vs-<opener>-4bet` |
| vs4bet | 3-bettor facing the opener's 4-bet jam | `<3bettor>-3bet-vs-<opener>-4bet-jam` |
| vs4bet | Opener facing the 3-bettor's 5-bet jam after its small 4-bet | `<opener>-4bet-vs-<3bettor>-jam` |
| squeeze | Any other decision in a pot where someone called raise 1 | generic |
| vs3bet (cold) | A seat that has not acted, facing an open and a non-all-in 3-bet | generic |
| vs4bet | Any other decision after raise 3 (cold 4-bet jams and the answers to them) | generic |
| vsShove | Any other decision facing a jam | generic |

The **generic id** is `<hero>-at-` followed by every non-fold action of the public line as `<seat>-<verb>`, with the
verbs open, limp, iso, call, 3bet, squeeze, 4bet, jam, reraise, check: `bb-at-co-open-btn-call` is the BB facing a
CO open and a BTN flat, `hj-at-utg-open-hj-3bet-co-jam` the 3-bettor facing a cold jam. It is unique by construction
(the queue is deterministic, so the non-fold actions fix every fold in between) and asserted anyway.

**Cold nodes.** A seat that has not acted, facing an open and a non-all-in 3-bet, may only fold or jam (R5). Such a
node is kind `vs3bet` with the generic id, the title "`<HERO>` — `<line>` (콜드: 폴드·올인)" and one action
`{ key: "shove", label: "콜드 올인", color: "allin" }`. Its description says "3벳 뒤 콜드 자리는 폴드나 올인만 해요 (트리
제약: 콜·작은 4벳 없음)." There are 84 such nodes with occ ≥ 0.1% at 100bb. They form their own group of the size
ladder, next to squeeze, vs4bet and vsShove, and PF-DATA-1 counts them as vs3bet.

**Actions.** Keys and labels carry the stage's real sizes: `open` "오픈 2.2bb" (the SB first in: key `raise`, next
to `limp`, labelled "오픈 3bb" as in cash6 and in every later spot of its line, "SB 오픈 후 vs BB 3벳"), `limp` "림프", `raise` "레이즈 3.5bb" for an iso and "리레이즈 10.5bb" for the SB's re-raise, `3bet`
"3벳 6.6bb", `squeeze` "스퀴즈 …bb", `4bet` "4벳 15.2bb", `call` "콜" (`overcall` "오버콜" when someone has already
called the open), `check` "체크", `shove` "올인". Raises use the color "raise", calls, limps and checks "call", jams
"allin". The BB facing an SB limp gets an explicit check that takes every hand not raised or jammed, so fold comes
out 0 and the 폴드 button is dropped (`noFold`).

**Titles and chips** follow the existing sets: "BB vs BTN 오픈", "CO 오픈 후 vs BTN 3벳", "BTN 3벳 후 vs CO 4벳 올인";
a multiway spot where the hero has not acted reads "BB vs CO 오픈 + BTN 콜"; other generic spots "HJ — UTG 오픈 → HJ
3벳 → CO 올인". A generic chip is the line without the hero ("CO오픈·BTN콜"), unambiguous once the seat filter has
picked the hero; where a group is small enough to show every seat at once and two chips read the same, the charts
page puts the hero in front of them ("SB: BTN오픈·SB3벳·BB올인", `displayChips`). Titles, chips, `line` and the
description's line show the fold of a seat that had already put voluntary chips in ("BB — CO 오픈 → SB 올인 → CO
폴드": the BB's call is heads-up, CO's chips are dead); other folds stay out, and the generic id never has folds.

### 6.5 Snapping and the size ladder

**Snapping.** Per class, the raw σ̄ over all actions, fold included, is floored to tenths and the remaining tenths go
out by largest remainder; ties go to the earlier action in the tree's order, fold last (`snapTenths` through
`snapStrategy`, which passes the tree's order; the solve.ts comment still says "display order"). The two orders
differ only at `bb-vs-sb-limp` (tree: check, raise; display: raise, check), and the draft data has no exact tie
there, so no cell depends on it. The same order is used for the gates' snapped gain and for the export. Every value is a
multiple of 0.1 and they sum to exactly 1, so buildScenario's "earlier action takes first" fill reproduces them. This
is the existing sets' 0.1 grid. `rangeText` writes compact range strings ("TT+, 99:0.5, A2s+, KTs-K6s:0.3") that
`parseRange` reads back to exactly the same numbers. Snapping costs exploitability: the snapped gain is 2.2 mbb/hand
at 100bb against 0.9 for σ̄ (§5.5).

**Size ladder.** Every rfi node is exported, and every other node whose occ reaches its threshold. rfi, vsOpen, vsLimp
and non-cold vs3bet use 0.1% of dealt hands. The ladder groups (squeeze, vs4bet, vsShove and the cold vs3bet nodes)
start at 0.1% and step 0.1 → 0.2 → 0.5 → 1.0% until the stage meets PF-DATA-1's counts (`chooseLadder`). Each group
moves on its own, only as far as its own count needs: a group whose own kind is over 72 steps up (the cold group when
vs3bet is over), and if only the total is over 180 every group below the top steps up. This per-group ladder is the
default the main session approved. One shared step would have to reach 1% at 100bb just to cut the 84 cold nodes
(36 + 84 vs3bet > 72), which drops every vs4bet chart; `--ladder shared` moves all groups together for comparison.
Then, while the gzip of the whole file is above 80,000 bytes, every group of every stage steps up together
(`raiseAll`); if it never fits, PF-SIZE-1 fails. The thresholds are written per kind to `occThresholdByKind` (vs3bet
carries the non-cold threshold, which every vs3bet scenario meets), the cold group's own threshold to
`occThresholdCold` (the data test checks every cold spot against it), and the lowest, 0.1, to `occThreshold`. The
ladder is deterministic: the same occ gives the same steps.

### 6.6 Confidence (신뢰도) and the sensitivity solves

Each shipped stage is solved five more times, 1,000 iterations each from a cold start, with one assumption changed
(`SENSITIVITY_VARIANTS`, `stageValuation`):

| Solve | Change | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Every uncalibrated parameter × 0.5: c outside (SRP, IP aggressor), multiway c_pos, the multiway u term, α_mw | the priors of §3.8 |
| (b) | The same × 1.5 | |
| (c) | Model B on the first 3 features (pair, suited, hi2f5), α held at 0.553, its own c −0.089 and θ (`alternatives.modelB3`) | the feature set; the fold-boundary extrapolation |
| (d) | The equity-scaled surplus form, 9 features, on every heads-up flop (`alternatives.product`, R per class) | the model form; the fold-boundary extrapolation |
| (e) | The old low-SPR rule: SPR_ALPHA = 6, so α shrinks with c and u | the 4-bet / jam split |

Solve (d) rests on two assumptions decided for v1, because the fit gives the surplus form only in the one fitted
context and no rule for the others:
1. **By position, not by role.** O, whoever raised, uses R_O of its class and I uses R_I of its class:
   p_O = R_O(h), p_I = R_I(x) − 1, q_O = 0, q_I = R_I(x) − 1.
2. **The same SPR shrink as model B, on the surplus part:** R → 1 + s · (R − 1) with s = min(1, SPR / 6).

Multiway flops stay model B in (d).

Solves (c) and (d) read `EQR_FIT.alternatives`: (c) its own c and the 3-long θ, applied to the first three columns
of the 9-feature matrix (`prepareModelB` uses the first θ.length columns); (d) the per-class R_O and R_I the fit
computed from all 9 features. Measured on one full 100bb-2.2 run of the shipped model (scratch copy, 1,000
iterations each): max gain (a) 2.10, (b) 3.02, (c) 2.66, (d) 1.93, (e) 2.29 mbb/hand; median move 6.4 / 4.9 / 8.2 /
5.4 / 3.0pp.

Per exported scenario, `move` is the largest over the five solves of the ρ-weighted mean total-variation distance
between the main solve's and the variant's action distributions, in pp (ρ = the main solve's raw own reach).

| Badge | Rule |
|---|---|
| 높음 (high) | move < 8 |
| 보통 (mid) | 8 ≤ move ≤ 20 |
| 낮음 (low) | move > 20, or flagged by PF-SANITY-5, PF-SANITY-6, PF-SANITY-7 or PF-TWOSTART |

The rule reads the stored move (rounded to 0.1pp), the number the data and the description show, so a raw 7.95 is
stored as 8 and badged 보통. Every vs3bet and vs4bet scenario at 55bb and more is badged at most 보통; its
description adds "작은 4벳과 4벳 올인의 배분은 낮은 SPR 가정에 민감해요." where the hero chooses between a small 4-bet and
a jam, and "3벳·4벳 팟은 낮은 SPR 가정에 민감해서 신뢰도를 보통까지만 줘요." elsewhere (cold spots, call-or-jam spots,
the answers to 4-bets). The description ends with the badge and its reason, for example "신뢰도 보통: 실현율 가정을
바꿔 풀면 이 차트가 약 14.2%p 움직여요." A run without sensitivity solves writes move 0, takes the badge from the checks
that ran but never above 보통, and says so ("신뢰도 보통(초안): 민감도 풀이를 아직 안 해서 움직임은 재지 않았어요.").
A stage is a draft (listed in `PREFLOP_DRAFT_STAGES`, its description adding "초안이에요: … 전이라 신뢰도는
임시예요.") when the sensitivity or two-start solves did not run, the gates were ignored, `--iterations` set any
schedule but §4.6's, or the two-start solve failed its own gates. The data test requires every non-draft stage to
have run at least 1,500 iterations.

### 6.7 Chart set texts

### 6.9 Close calls

Many 100% cells are EV ties (§8): the chart rounds σ̄ to tenths, and where two actions are worth the same the solver's
pick between them is arbitrary. The data marks these cells so neither the charts page nor the quiz presents a tie as a
clear preference.

**The quantity.** For a hand h that reaches the spot, B^a(h) for every legend action a (the scenario's actions in
order, then fold unless `noFold`): the best-response action value against σ̄, the unsnapped average the chart rounds.
The hero best-responds below the node and every other seat plays σ̄; bb net from the start of the hand, per arrival
(given that the hero holds h at the node), so the hero's own reach does not enter the comparison.
`solver.evaluate(σ̄, { brActionValues: true })` returns it per edge (§4). B rather than V^a (the hero following σ̄
below) because a line the hero would play differently below must be valued at its best; σ̄ rather than the snapped
profile because the snapped profile's off-grid rounding moves the opponents' replies. Checked by hand: at 100bb-2.2
AA's call is worth 68.3823 bb against the engine's 68.38233, fold −11.5.

**Eligible actions.** Fold always; a non-fold action only when it takes at least 1% of the reaching range
(`reachShares`, the legend total the page shows). Below 1% its subtree is off σ̄'s path and the opponents' replies
there have not converged (§8, "A 0% action can be a non-credible-threat artifact"): near sets that relied on such an
action (37 / 27 / 39 / 97 cells) kept their set against the two-start solve only 55% of the time, against 91.5%
overall. Example: 35bb `utgp1-vs-utg-open` KQs would be {call, 3-bet} with the call used 0.000%.

**The near set and the mark.** near(h) = the eligible actions with B ≥ best − ε, best the maximum over the eligible
actions, ε = 0.05 bb, inclusive. A cell is marked when the chart shows it pure (one legend action at ≥ 0.999), near(h)
has at least two actions, and the chart's action is one of them. Mixed cells are never marked: the chart already shows
their mix, and marks on every cell do not fit PF-SIZE-1 in a release run. A near set without the chart's action
(e.g. 100bb-3 `bb-at-utgp2-open-btn-call` AJo, near {squeeze, fold} while the chart overcalls 50%) would contradict the
chart, so it is never written; pure cells whose action is ε or more below the best eligible one are not marked either
(2 / 0 / 0 / 0: 100bb-2.2 `hj-at-utgp1-open-lj-call` folds 54s and 65s, 0.125 and 0.127 bb below the squeeze; counting
any cell's top chart action, mixed cells included, gives 25 / 23 / 11 / 4).

**Why ε = 0.05.** Its stability: the marks keep the same set in 95.4 / 91.4 / 87.2% of the 100bb-2.2 cells against
scratch solves at 1,750 and 1,500 iterations and the two-start solve, 94.8 / 90.8% at 100bb-3, 95.2% at 55bb and
95.0% at 35bb against 1,250 iterations; flips sit at the ε boundary. And its size: 0.05 bb per arrival is below any
difference a pub player acts on. It equals `DEFAULT_GATES.localRegret` numerically, but that gate is a reach-weighted
node average of max_a V − Σσ·V, not a per-hand best-response gap, so the equality is no argument for it.

**Encoding and size.** One 169-character string per scenario in `ALL_HANDS` order: "." or a lowercase hex digit, the
bitmask over the legend (at most 4 actions; the generator throws above). Omitted when nothing is marked; `closeEps` per
stage. Measured alternatives: in-reach characters only (−1.1K gzip), a delta against the chart's own mixes (+8.5K,
which would also mark mixed cells), sparse range strings; the 169-character form is self-describing and fits.
Annotations add 8,265 bytes gzip to the draft (72,215 of 80,000); a release run is projected near 77,500. If a refit
pushes the annotated file over PF-SIZE-1, the generator drops close strings from the rarest spots first, stepping the
threshold through 0.2, 0.5, 1 and 2% of dealt hands (`CLOSE_LADDER`; no 0.1 step, since every shipped spot has occ ≥
0.1, and a step that drops nothing is skipped) and printing "PF-SIZE-1: annotated gzip X > 80,000; close strings kept
only on spots with occ ≥ T% (N spots lost theirs)". `dropClose` records the step as `closeMinOcc` on each stage that
lost a string and recomputes that stage's flags on the strings it keeps: a cell whose mark is gone is a clear cell
again, so a rule hit there joins its flag (on the committed draft, 0.5% turns `utgp1-open-vs-bb-3bet` KK into a
PF-SANITY-12 flag; 2% changes 4 spots). The generator's self-check `flagProblems` re-derives every stage's flags from
its stored close strings, as the data test does. Only if 2% still does not fit does the run fail ("PF-SIZE-1 FAILED
after annotations", nothing written). Spots never change, and PF-SIZE-1's limit is unchanged.

**What a mark means, and what it does not.** A mark is true of the model against σ̄: 87–95% of marks survive the other
checkpoints and the two-start solve. Its absence is not a clear preference everywhere: in 100bb all-in spots the
equity table's ±0.45pp (§4.10) is about ±0.9 bb in a 200bb pot, far above ε, so an unmarked cell there can be a tie in
the real game (100bb-3 `bb-3bet-vs-btn-4bet-jam` 88: call − fold −0.39 against the snapped jam range). Mixed cells
carry no mark; the quiz grades their played actions by the solver set's mixed floor instead (§6.8).

### 6.10 Artifact flags

Some cells show a known model or tree artifact (§8) as a clear model preference. The data flags them per scenario as
`{ code, hands, vs? }`, range strings with every value 1, in code order 9, 11, 12, 13, 14 (numeric, not string order).
"Raise" is open, raise, 3-bet, squeeze, 4-bet or jam, "plays" is 1 − fold, every comparison has 1e-9 slack, and a cell
is a tie when the chart shows it mixed or it carries a close mark. `hands` lists only rule hits on clear cells (pure,
unmarked), so every listed hand is a clear preference in the artifact's direction against the eligible actions (an
action under 1% of the reaching range can still be within ε: in 3 of the 10 -12 KK flags the unused small 4-bet is
0.013–0.045 bb behind); a flag is written only when `hands` is not empty. `vs` lists the comparison hands, measured against the remaining `hands`, on any cell (the caution
quotes them).

are within 0.045 bb and close-marked, and 36 of the 43 TT–77 `vs` cells of PF-SANITY-11 are, with a median gap of

The quiz never deals `hands`, nor the `vs` of -9 and -14 (both sides distorted, §8); it keeps the `vs` of -11 (TT–77
against opens, where folding is a real error) and -13 (AA's small 4-bet, the line §8 recommends). That skips 54 / 61 /

cell is a tie (at 100bb-2.2, 19 of the 28 -9 `vs` cells, A5s–A2s, are close-marked or mixed within ε): the artifact

leave out the line a corrected model would choose (a {call, fold} A2s tie would grade the 3-bet 오답). Dealing them back
when the cell is a tie would restore a core 3-bet-bluff drill at the cost of grading it against a distorted value; a
graded wheel term (§8) is the fix that brings them back. Flags never change a badge, a frequency or a
description; PF-SANITY-11 to -14 also print every unrestricted hit (§5.2).

## 7. How to regenerate

```
node scripts/fit-realization.mjs              # refit model B, its alternatives and κ → src/poker/preflop/eqrFit.ts
node scripts/fit-realization.mjs --check      # recompute and compare with the committed fit; exit 1 if it differs
node scripts/fit-realization.mjs --packs a,b  # fit on these manifest packs only (default: all of them)
node scripts/make-preflop.mjs                 # every stage with two-start and sensitivity solves → preflopData.ts
node scripts/make-preflop.mjs --check         # the same plus PF-DET; compares with the committed file, writes nothing
```

The fit comes first because the charts depend on it. `make-preflop.mjs --check` is how a reviewer confirms that the
committed data is exactly what the committed code, fit and table give. Useful flags: `--stages a,b` (a subset),
`--iterations N` (cap the schedule and the sensitivity solves, for quick tests; gates still apply; a draft unless
N is 4,000),
`--no-sensitivity` and `--no-twostart` (a draft: listed in `PREFLOP_DRAFT_STAGES`, badges provisional, said so in the
descriptions), `--ignore-gates` (development only), `--dump DIR` (per stage the config, σ̄ as float32 and every
node's line, kind, occ, reach, regret and shares, for review), `--summary-dir DIR`, `--workers N`, `--out FILE`. The
main solves alone took 246 s on 4 workers (§4.7; the latest draft, 5 stages with the 35bb re-solve, 309 s); the
release run adds one two-start and five 1,000-iteration solves per shipped stage.

Things to know before regenerating:
- **The fit follows the packs.** `fit-realization.mjs` reads every flop file listed in `public/packs/manifest.json`
  (or of the `--packs` subset), and the presolve workflow adds flops every 6 hours on 16 shards
  (`docs/solver-packs.md`). A new pack, such as a UTG-vs-BB one, joins the default fit as soon as the manifest lists
  it; `--packs` leaves it out (or picks it) deliberately. Once the packs have grown,
  a refit gives a different fit hash and different charts, and `fit-realization.mjs --check` exits 1: that means the
  packs moved, not that the fit is broken. `eqrFit.ts` records each pack's version, flop count and file-list hash, so
  the pack state behind a chart is always known. Refit deliberately (v1.5, when both packs cover all 1,755 flop
  classes), not after every presolve commit.
- **Pack changes that break the fit.** A pack whose OOP may bet the flop stops the fit (IP's root EV is not stored,
  §3.7); a pack version bump changes the fit like new flops do.
- **The equity table** is read from `node_modules/.cache/pubaj-equity-12000.json`; when it is missing,
  `scripts/equity-cache.mjs` rebuilds it (about 40 s on 4 cores, the same table on any machine, §4.10).
  `make-preflop.mjs` stops when the table's sha256 is not the one the fit was made with (`--ignore-gates` lets a
development run through), and the data test pins both hashes to `eqrFit.ts`.
- **Tree changes** show up first as a failing count snapshot in `tree.test.ts` (§2.5); update it deliberately.

## 8. Limitations

- **The caps of §2.6.** No cold call or small cold 4-bet behind a 3-bet, one caller per 3-bet, at most 3–4 players
  to a flop, one size per raise level, no open limps. No 3-way all-ins: PF-FROZEN puts the lines where the first
  call of a jam folds out a seat with voluntary chips at 0.7% of hands at 100bb, 1.6% at 55bb and 1.1% at 35bb.
- **The mass method** (§4.3): pairwise card removal, no bunching, and with 3+ seats not one well-defined game.
  Σ EV ≈ +0.12–0.13 bb/hand; per-seat EVs are each seat's own-world EV. Gains and NashConv stay valid in each seat's
  own world.
- **Postflop is modelled, not solved.** One context (SRP, BTN open vs BB call) is fitted. Everything else in §3.8 is
  a documented prior: OOP aggressors, limped pots, IP callers, the SPR shrinks of 3-bet and 4-bet pots, the low-SPR α
  rule that decides the 4-bet / jam split, and all of multiway (κ product, α_mw = α, c_pos ±0.02), which our heads-up
  packs can never calibrate. Model B is linear in equity, so a weak hand against a strong range can get a negative
  pot share (counted, at most 0.027% of hands, at 100bb-3).
- **The packs' own biases** (§3.7): no OOP donk bet and a coarse IP tree, a 6-max no-ante cash pack, hand-made pack
  ranges, so the fold-boundary realization that decides BB and SB defense is extrapolated.
- **Chip EV, not ICM.** Nothing here knows about pay jumps or the bubble; `docs/game-profile.md` shows how much ICM
  changes calls at the target game's payouts.
- **Equal stacks**, 9 seats, a 1bb BB ante, no rake, no straddle. 169 hand classes with average card removal.
- **Convergence.** 9-player DCFR has no convergence or uniqueness guarantee. The gates bound each seat's gain at the
  end of the run, PF-TWOSTART probes for a second equilibrium, and rare lines below the export thresholds may be
  poorly converged. Snapping to the 0.1 grid costs up to about 1.9 mbb/hand (100bb-3).

Known artifacts a strong player will see in the charts (found in review; none is an export bug). The best-response
values quoted below are bb per hand class at 100bb-2.2, the actor best-responding from the node down and every other
seat at σ̄; they come from a re-solve (2,000 iterations, the same gates as the draft, 0.899 / 4.513) whose
evaluation also exported each action's best-response value. That export is now in the engine,
`evaluate(σ̄, { brActionValues: true })` (§4, tested in `solve.test.ts`), and the generator writes the close marks
(§6.9) from it. The artifacts below that a rule can find are flagged in the data (§6.10): the charts page shows their
caution, and the quiz skips the flagged cells.
- **Top-card inversions (PF-SANITY-8, print only).** In RFI and BB defense the kicker inversions of the 5-feature
  fit (weak Ax below the Kx with the same kicker, KTo / JTo below K9o / J9o) stay gone: PF-SANITY-7 is 0 on every
  stage (§3.7 bias 5). Model B's u is additive and blind to the opponent's range, so against tight ranges a Kx < Qx
  cell can remain: 55bb UTG+2 KTo 0.1 < QTo 0.3 and 35bb UTG+2 KTo 0.5 < QTo 0.8 (0 / 1 / 3 / 3 such spots before,
  0 / 0 / 1 / 1 now; §5.5). The model now over-states the IP K − Q step (§3.7 bias 5), which favours Kx, so a Qx cell
  well below its Kx is the direction to suspect; nothing checks it. A few Q-x below J-x cells exist too, unchecked, in
  the direction the packs' own shares point.
- **Kicker, top-card and same-gap inversions outside RFI and BB defense (PF-SANITY-10, print only).** The kicker fix
  does not reach the squeezes: Ax-below-Kx pairs there are 60 / 43 / 23 / 22 per stage (74 / 36 / 18 / 31 with the
  previous fit), e.g. at 100bb-2.2 `hj-at-utg-open-lj-call` folds ATs and squeezes KTs 100%, and `sb-vs-btn-open` (occ
  12.0%) folds A7o and calls K7o. Kx-below-Qx pairs in squeezes fell (20 / 19 / 11 / 25, 35 / 25 / 17 / 31 before), and
  same-gap inversions outside RFI and BB defense fell at 100bb-2.2 and 35bb (76 and 56, 107 and 65 before) but grew at
  100bb-3 (96, 64 before; `btn-at-lj-open-co-call` squeezes 65s and folds 76s). RFI has none any more (UTG at 100bb-3
  folds 54s, 65s and 76s alike). Counts per kind in §5.5. By best response the squeeze hits are real model
  preferences, not ties: `btn-at-utg-open-co-call` (occ 0.85%) loses 0.44 bb squeezing A9s and gains 0.02 squeezing
  K9s, ATs −0.09 against KTs +0.43; the cause is not traced (blockers and the squeeze-pot valuation are untested).
  The cold vs-open hits are mostly ties: `lj-vs-utg-open` 3-bets K9s 58% at −0.008 bb and A9s 15% at +0.001.
- **3-bet bluffs on low suited connectors, not wheel aces (PF-SANITY-9, print only): fewer, not gone.** At 100bb-2.2
  `co-vs-utg-open` (occ 9.2%) 3-bets 54s and 65s 100% and 43s 0% (100% before), 3-bets A4s 50%, flats A5s and folds
  A3s, A2s and 55; `hj-vs-utg-open` 3-bets 54s 100% and 65s and A5s 50%; `bb-vs-utg-open` 3-bets 54s 80% and 43s 20%
  and flats A5s–A2s. Across the 100bb-2.2 vs-open and first-in squeeze spots the 3-bet frequency summed over 43s,
  54s, 65s, 53s and 64s is 32.2 hand classes (47.5 before) against 20.4 over A5s–A2s (19.2). PF-SANITY-9 fires on 16
  spots at 100bb-2.2 and 9 at 100bb-3 (23 and 16 before), none at 55bb or 35bb. The aggressor's low-card
  over-prediction behind it is fixed where the packs have rows (§3.7 bias 8; u_aggr(43s) − u_aggr(A5s) = +0.218, the
  cash rows +0.213), so what remains comes from applying u_aggr to every 3-bettor and u_call to every cold caller (the
  role rule, §3.8), and from u ignoring the opener's range. By best response at 100bb-2.2, `co-vs-utg-open` gains
  0.133 bb by 3-betting 54s, while A5s gains 0.062 by calling and 0.040 by 3-betting, and A2s loses 0.058 by calling.
  Against UTG's range that continues against a CO 3-bet, A5s has more equity than 54s (0.366 against 0.308) and
  leaves fewer combos (90.5 against 102.5), yet u_aggr(54s) − u_aggr(A5s) is +0.218 of the pot whatever the range:
  that gap was fitted against the BB's wide range, where the equity gap is larger (0.569 against 0.394 vs the cash BB
  range). In `bb-vs-utg-open` the 54s 3-bet (80%) is an exact tie (0.000 bb), and A5s flats by 0.195 bb because the
  OOP 3-bettor gets u_aggr (A5s −0.070, against u_call −0.013). No feature-level fix exists: the lead is a u that
  depends on the opponent's range (the product form of sensitivity solve (d) is one), and the UTG pack is the first
  data on it. A strong player still reads "CO vs UTG: 3-bet 54s, flat A5s" as wrong. Against UTG–LJ openers the clear
  cells are flagged (7 / 5 / 0 / 0 spots, §6.10): the page shows the caution and the quiz skips the connectors and the
  wheel aces there.
- **Wide BB defense with low-card trash.** BB defense vs BTN / SB is 82.3 / 90.8% at 100bb-2.2 (84.0 / 92.9 before)
  and 80.5 / 90.6% at 55bb (84.5 / 92.8), because the rank features still give 7-high and 3-high hands more
  realization than K- and Q-high trash (OOP EQR 72o 0.46, K2o 0.55; §3.7), as the packs' own rows down to 5-high do.
  At 100bb-2.2 the BB defends K2o and Q2o vs the BTN but folds 72o, 32o and T2o. These hands are outside both packs'
  BB ranges, so this is extrapolation (flat below 5-high now, §3.4). The BTN no longer opens 32s, 42s or 52s (43s
  still opens) and opens A4o 40% (a tie, 0.000 bb), but still folds A2o and A3o (−0.18 and −0.09 bb by best
  response) and K2o–K4o while opening K5o, Q6o and J7o (+0.06, +0.01, +0.01). The offsuit aces follow measured rows
  (cash IP EQR 0.88–0.93 for A2o–A5o, the model within 0.01); K5o, Q6o and J7o have no IP rows and are extrapolated
  from K6o, Q8o and J8o.
- **BB vs UTG Q-x.** At 100bb-2.2 the BB calls Q7s 70%, Q6s 60% and QTo 80% against a UTG open (all folded before)
  but still folds Q5s–Q2s and Q9o while defending 73s, 63s, 62s, 52s, 42s and 32s 100%: u does not depend on the
  opener's range, so the domination of Q-x by a tight range is not modelled (the packs' OOP 73s realizes at least as
  well as Q7s against the wide BTN range). The UTG pack (`pub-srp-utg-bb-55`, solving now) will measure it. By best
  response the margins over folding are small: 62s +0.028, 32s +0.036 and 84s +0.032 bb defended, Q5s −0.036, Q2s
  −0.132 and J5s −0.127 folded. The OOP top-card curve adds to it: hi2f5 under-rates J-x and Q-x against 7- to 9-high
  hands (§3.7 bias 5), so `bb-vs-utgp2-open` folds J3s (−0.019) and defends 93s, 83s and 73s (+0.034, +0.021,
  +0.079).
- **Small pairs and weak suited aces at early seats (100bb-2.2).** UTG folds 22–55 and opens 66 20%, HJ folds 22–44,
  the CO folds 22–33 (55bb: CO folds 22); UTG opens A9s 100%, A8s 20% and A5s 50% and folds A7s, A6s, A4s–A2s. These
  are clear preferences, not ties: by best response opening 22 / 33 / 44 / 55 at UTG loses 0.48 / 0.41 / 0.33 / 0.18
  bb (66 is a tie, +0.004), at the HJ 22 / 33 / 44 lose 0.39 / 0.25 / 0.06 and at the CO 22 / 33 lose 0.24 / 0.04. The
  model's pair curve is convex where the packs' is flat from 22 to 55 (§3.7 bias 9): IP 22 realizes 1.02 in the model
  against 1.14 measured, the worst of all pairs. The cause is α held at the prior, not the pair term's shape: the
  same features with α free give IP 22 1.12, and no monotone pair curve with α held gets 22 above 1.08.
- **More KK flats.** The pair-rank slope that makes QQ–AA realize well as callers (§3.7 bias 7) lets KK flat 3-bets:
  at 100bb-2.2 the UTG opener calls a blind 3-bet with KK 100% and UTG+1 / UTG+2 80–100% / 30–70% (9 KK cells below a
  full raise across vs-open and vs-3-bet spots, 10 before), at 100bb-3 the UTG opener calls 3-bets with KK 80–100% of
  the time from every seat (21 cells, 21 before), at 55bb-2.2 9 cells (10) and at 35bb none. Against opens the same
slope leaves KK's flat within ε of its 3-bet in 9 pure 3-bet cells (6 at 100bb-2.2, 3 at 100bb-3, e.g. `co-vs-utg-open`
call 7.224 against 3-bet 7.256): they carry close marks, but the quiz does not accept the flat and the hand card adds
the big-pair caveat (§6.8). AA raises wherever it
  can except the SB first in, which limps AA 100% at 100bb-2.2, 100bb-3 and 55bb-2.2 (occ 9–11%) and KK 90 / 90 / 40%.
- **Jams over small 4-bets and cold calls.** PF-SANITY-6 fires on 12 spots at 100bb-2.2 (11 before), all against
  blind 3-bets, on 27 at 100bb-3 and 21 at 55bb; PF-SANITY-5 on 4 / 1 / 7 / 2 spots (BTN and CO cold calls of 10–16%
  against early opens). Both follow from α held at the prior and the role rule (§3.8); all are badged 낮음.
- **35bb ships without cold jams.** The cold4 "jam" solve now passes every gate (gain 0.328, NashConv 1.95, snapped
  1.08 at 1,500) but is still the degenerate profile: UTG never min-raises and opens 4.6% by jamming, against
  off-path 3-bets of 21–42% vs a UTG open, which leaves 28 vs-open spots, below PF-DATA-1's 30 (§5.5). The generator
  re-solves such a stage with cold4 "none" (§2.8), so 35bb-2 is that re-solve, the only usable 35bb for now: the cold
  seat behind a 3-bet folds. Players in front of that seat see the fold-only cap's signature (the middle column of
  §2.8, where UTG+1 vs UTG called 0.6 and 3-bet 8.7%): cold flats all but vanish and 3-bets inflate (shares of all
  hands, against the 5-feature fit's cold-jam 35bb: UTG+1 vs UTG call 0.0 / 3-bet 8.4 against 7.1 / 4.1; HJ vs LJ

  vs BTN 3-bet 21.9 against 11.5 plus 7.3 jams). Cold calls jump between seats (the BTN calls T9s and 98s 100% vs a

  `lj-vs-utg-open`, occ 12.5–14.9%, fold ATs and 3-bet KTs), and the BTN's RFI falls to 45.4% (51.9% at 55bb). Until a
  cold-jam 35bb can be solved, these spots are the weakest charts in the set; whether the quiz should skip or badge
  the 35bb non-blind vs-open spots is open.
- **A 0% action can be a non-credible-threat artifact.** A line nobody takes has reach ≈ 0, so the regrets behind it
  keep their early values: in the previous fit's 35bb-2 draft `lj-vs-utg-open` the LJ never flatted (fold 93.1 /
  call 0 / 3-bet 6.9) while its neighbours flatted 6.7–8.2%, because after an LJ call the BTN squeezed 46% and the CO
